    Retraining cannot promise zero bypasses. Construction can: add one rule that fires when every word of the claim is present, then let the solver find the smallest weight that makes the claim provable.

      How it works

      1. A guard you can read. The TRACE guard is a Tsetlin machine: 200 rules, each an AND of words that must be present or absent. 100 vote unsafe, 100 vote safe, and a prompt is blocked when the unsafe votes win. It scores 84% on the held-out split of deepset/prompt-injections.
      2. Logic, not samples. Each rule becomes a gate and the vote becomes a counter: about 21,000 variables and 48,000 clauses. A claim is true exactly when its negation has no solution.
      3. A solver in a tab. A conflict-driven clause-learning solver, written for this page: watched literals, clause learning, activity-based branching and restarts, in a Web Worker.
      4. Don’t trust it. Check it. Every clause the solver learns is re-derived by unit propagation, independently of the solver. Download the formula and the proof and run drat-trim on them yourself.

      What a proof here does not say

      • It is about words, not meaning. A paraphrase that avoids the words is a different claim, and this small guard misses many of them.
      • A repair has a cost: the new rule blocks every prompt that contains all of its words, harmless ones included. The repair card counts the verdicts it changes on 662 labelled prompts.
      • The guard is small on purpose. Readable models trade accuracy for proofs; the VERITAS paper measures that trade-off. This page reimplements the method in JavaScript on the TRACE guard; the paper’s experiments use the Python package with z3 and PySAT.
